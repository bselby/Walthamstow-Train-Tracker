Privacy

This app is a handful of static files served from a CDN, plus two small functions. One, used only on the Suffragette viewpoint, is stateless and proxies freight-data requests to Realtime Trains so that their API credentials stay server-side. The other stores the "train is passing" taps described below. Both log requests as part of normal Netlify operation; neither sees your coordinates or who you are. Plus a privacy-friendly pageview count via Cloudflare Web Analytics (details below). Apart from a tap on a "train is passing" button, and which viewpoint's page you open (part of the pageview count), nothing you do inside the app — tapping the fact line, turning on location, starring a viewpoint, watching the countdown — is tracked or reported back to me.

What's stored on your device

  • A single localStorage flag (wtt_walking_enabled) that remembers whether you've turned location on (for sorting spots by distance and showing walking times).
  • A wtt_favourites list of your starred viewpoints, in the order they were starred. It is saved the first time the app opens, even if empty. If you used the app before stars became a list, it starts with the viewpoint you had starred, or East Avenue if you had none, and the older wtt_favourite_viewpoint flag is removed.
  • A wtt_fact_index number, so the fact line doesn't start on the same fact every time.
  • A wtt_tap_state record for the "train is passing" button: whether the buttons are on or off (with when you last switched them off or said "Not now", and how many times you've answered or changed it), how many trains you've booked, a short key for each of the last 20 trains you booked (viewpoint, direction and predicted time, so the same train isn't counted twice), the times of your latest tap so its register entry can be shown, and a random id made on your device (see below).
  • A wtt_seen_freight_regions list in sessionStorage (cleared when the tab closes), noting which freight regions have already been shown so a new one can be highlighted once.
  • The PWA's service worker caches the app shell (HTML, CSS, JS, icons) so the app works offline. None of this is shared with me.

"Train is passing" taps

At some viewpoints a button appears as a train is due. If you tap it, your browser sends one small record to this site's own function, which stores it so the bridge timings can be tuned. A tap sends exactly these fields:

  • viewpointId and direction — which spot and which way the train was going.
  • msSinceTap — how long ago you tapped when the record was sent, in milliseconds, so the server can work out the tap time.
  • predictedAtShowMs and predictedAtTapMs — when the app predicted the train when the button appeared and when you tapped.
  • source — whether that prediction came from signalling data (berth) or TfL (tfl).
  • berthStepMs — when the signalling step behind the prediction fired, if there was one.
  • tflExpectedArrivalMs — TfL's expected arrival time for that train, if known.
  • vehicleId and berthTrainId — TfL's and the signalling system's ids for the train, if known.
  • atViewpoint — just yes, no or unknown: whether you were within about 150 metres of the viewpoint. It is unknown unless location is on and your phone has found its location.
  • deviceId — a random id made on your device and kept in wtt_tap_state, so repeat taps from one phone can be told apart. It isn't linked to you.

No coordinates are sent, and there is no account. The server adds the time it received the tap and the tap time worked out from it. Taps are kept for 12 months and then deleted. If you choose "Not now", or switch the buttons off from the ticket under the train times, no buttons appear and nothing is sent.

Geolocation

If you tap Use my location on the home screen or Enable walking time on a spot, your browser hands your latitude/longitude to this app's JavaScript code. Those coordinates are used on your device only — to work out how far you are from each spot, so the list can be sorted nearest first and walking times shown — and are never transmitted anywhere. They are not stored, logged, or sent. A tap sends only whether you were near the viewpoint (yes or no), never the coordinates.

You can turn this off at any time using the small off link next to "Using your location" on the home screen or next to the walking label on a spot. That stops the location watch and removes the localStorage flag. The app never asks for your location until you tap one of those two buttons.

Third parties your browser talks to

Because the app runs entirely in your browser, a few network requests go to third parties. I don't see any of these, but they may log your IP and user agent on their own servers:

  • TfL Unified API (api.tfl.gov.uk) — for live train arrivals. TfL's own privacy policy covers what they do with their logs. The home screen asks TfL for arrivals at the stations behind the spots listed, about every 30 seconds while it is open.
  • Realtime Trains (via /.netlify/functions/freight) — only while a Suffragette-line spot's countdown is open (Queens Road Station or Northcote Road Bridge), never from the home screen. Their privacy policy covers what they log. My proxy forwards your browser's freight request to their API and returns their response unchanged; no user-identifying data is added.
  • Live signalling feed (e17trains-td-proxy.fly.dev, a small service I run on Fly.io) — only while a spot's countdown is open, never from the home screen. Your browser holds a connection open to it and receives train movements from Network Rail's signalling data, which sharpen the countdown. Your browser sends it nothing beyond the connection itself. The service records only that a connection opened or closed; Fly.io, which hosts it, may log your IP address.
  • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com) — to load the Big Shoulders typeface.
  • Netlify — the CDN that serves this site. Netlify logs requests as part of hosting the site, and stores the "train is passing" taps (via /.netlify/functions/tap).
  • Cloudflare — provides DNS for this domain and also runs the pageview analytics (see next section).

Analytics

This site uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to count how many people visit. It's designed around not identifying individual visitors:

  • No cookies. Nothing is stored in your browser by the analytics beacon.
  • No fingerprinting. No device, canvas, or font fingerprinting.
  • No cross-site tracking. Cloudflare doesn't build a profile of you across the web.
  • What it does see: the page URL, referrer (if any), user agent, and a coarse country estimate from your IP. Each viewpoint has its own URL (for example /walthamstow/east-avenue-bridge), so the count shows which viewpoints are opened.
  • What it doesn't see: anything else you do inside the app — whether location is on, the facts you tap, the viewpoints you star, the buttons you press.

Cloudflare publish their own privacy policy covering what they keep and for how long.

What I don't do

  • No advertising or ad-network code.
  • No account, login, or sign-up.
  • No cookies set by this site itself.
  • No third-party scripts beyond the Cloudflare pageview beacon and the Google Fonts loader.
